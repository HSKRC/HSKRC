Founding of a capital

The creation of al-Qahira

In 969 CE (358 AH), the Fatimid commander Jawhar al-Siqilli entered Egypt on the orders of the imam-caliph al-Muʿizz li-Din Allah. He established a new planned capital northeast of the existing urban settlements. The city came to be known as al-Qahira (Cairo).

Cairo was not a city without predecessors: al-Fustat and other earlier centres remained essential parts of Egypt’s urban history. The foundation of the new capital marked an important change in Fatimid state organisation and court life.

Urban heritage

Architecture and public life

The Fatimid foundation was associated with a walled city, ceremonial spaces and a network of streets. Al-Azhar was begun in 970 and became one of its most enduring institutions. Cairo later expanded far beyond its original Fatimid plan.
