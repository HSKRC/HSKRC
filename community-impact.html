Community Impact

HSKRC is an independent digital cultural and research project created for worldwide access. It preserves, organises and connects historical knowledge through a digital library, research articles, source-led knowledge pages, bilingual access and online research tools that can be used from anywhere in the world.

What has changed through HSKRC?

HSKRC has made historical resources easier to locate in one public archive, created bilingual reading routes and published a community heritage record. These are demonstrated outputs. The live counters below show activity; the effect on learning, research or heritage conservation requires separate evidence.

For readers and students

English and Persian introductions connect historical topics to sources and further reading. The benefit to each reader can be reported through the experience form.

For community contributors

Local accounts can be documented with attribution and visible research limits, instead of remaining an unpublished account.

For researchers

Bibliographic records, a source registry and article references provide routes to inspect evidence. No time-saving or research-quality improvement has yet been quantified here.

Evidence status

Published outputs and website activity are available. Evaluated learning outcomes, independently verified conservation results and an approved testimonial series are not yet published in this report.

Public Value & SDG Alignment

HSKRC keeps core research access free and connects its public value to education and cultural heritage.

SDG 4 · Quality Education

Improves access to organised research material, bilingual educational content and traceable scholarly sources.

SDG 11.4 · Cultural Heritage

Supports documentation, awareness and digital preservation of places, texts, people and historical records linked to Alamut and Nizari Ismaili heritage.

Global reach, with a local connection

HSKRC is designed for readers, researchers, students and communities worldwide. At the same time, the project is being developed in Victoria and can also contribute locally by making cultural and historical knowledge more accessible to diverse communities in and around Greater Dandenong.

HSKRC does not claim impact that has not yet been measured. As the project grows, worldwide usage, community participation, feedback, collaborations and local engagement will be documented here with verifiable evidence.

Three examples that can be checked

01 · Community heritage

From a local account to a public record

A contributor shared a family/community account and a banknote image. HSKRC assembled these into the bilingual Lakhshom Qala profile, distinguishing testimony from independently documented history.

Available evidence: public profile, attributed account and supplied image. Still needed: dated interviews, site photographs and archival corroboration. This record does not establish a conservation outcome.

Inspect the profile · Explore the collection
02 · Research traceability

From an unreferenced article to linked evidence

The Alamut guide was revised on 29 October 2026 in English and Persian with section headings, numbered references, seven bibliography entries and an editorial revision note.

Available evidence: both revised articles and their linked references. Readers can inspect the basis of claims. Independent scholarly peer review and reader comprehension gains have not yet been documented.

English article · Persian article
03 · A connected learning route

Historical context across cities and institutions

The Fatimid collection connects an era overview to Cairo, al-Azhar, cities, architecture, scholars and a source guide. Readers can move between a historical subject and its wider setting.

Available evidence: linked public pages and source guidance. This is a published educational resource; classroom adoption and learning outcomes have not yet been evaluated in this report.

Reader-reported benefitResource used, purpose and reported changeCollect through the private experience form. Label as self-reported; check examples or supporting material before publishing a case study.
Heritage documentationAttribution, dates, permissions and corroborationInspect the published record and identify unresolved claims. Publishing a profile alone does not demonstrate physical preservation.

Data limitations: cumulative traffic depends on the monitoring period and the provider’s collection and filtering rules. Refresh time is the time HSKRC retrieved the response, not an independent audit date. Missing or stale data must not be interpreted as zero activity.
