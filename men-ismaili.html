Ismaili da'i and Sulayhid ruler of Yemen

Ali al-Sulayhi

d. 1067 CE · Sulayhid state founded c. 1047

A Yemeni Ismaili missionary, Ali al-Sulayhi built a political base in the highlands and founded the Sulayhid dynasty, which recognised the Fatimid Imam-caliphs. He expanded the da'wa and ruled much of Yemen with the support of his wife and co-ruler Asma bint Shihab. He was killed in 1067 during a tribal conflict; his son Ahmad al-Mukarram succeeded him.

Fatimid chief da'i, poet and statesman

Al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din al-Shirazi

c. 1000–1078 CE

Born in Shiraz, al-Mu'ayyad first served Buyid rulers while promoting the Fatimid cause in western Iran. After political danger, he travelled to Cairo, where he eventually became head of the Ismaili da'wa under al-Mustansir. He also undertook diplomatic missions in Syria and Iraq. His Arabic memoir, Sira, is a rare first-person account of Fatimid political and missionary life.

Persian Ismaili missionary and philosopher

Abu Ya'qub al-Sijistani

d. c. 971 CE · birth details unknown

Al-Sijistani was a leading Ismaili da'i and philosopher in tenth-century Iran. He developed a distinctive intellectual account of creation, prophecy and religious authority, drawing on late antique philosophy while writing within the Ismaili tradition. His books, including Kitab al-Yanabi', became important sources for later Ismaili thought. Contemporary biographical records of his life are scarce.

Fatimid theologian and philosopher

Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani

fl. c. 996–1021 CE · birth date unknown

Of Persian origin, al-Kirmani flourished during the reign of al-Hakim. A senior missionary in Iraq and Iran, he was called to Cairo to address doctrinal disputes within the da'wa. He wrote major works on theology, philosophy and the Imamate, including Rahat al-'aql. Because he worked secretly in a hostile political climate, little is known about his family, childhood or final years.

Persian poet, philosopher and da'i

Nasir Khusraw

1004–1088 CE (traditional dates)

Born in Qubadiyan near Balkh, Nasir served in government before a spiritual crisis led him to travel through the Middle East. In Cairo he embraced Ismaili teaching and was appointed hujja for Khurasan. Returning east, he taught and wrote in Persian until opposition forced him into refuge in Yumgan, Badakhshan. His Safarnama records a seven-year journey; his philosophical poetry and prose shaped Ismaili communities in Central and South Asia.

Founder of the Nizari state at Alamut

Hasan-i Sabbah

c. 1050s–1124 CE

Born into a Twelver Shi'i family in Qumm, Hasan encountered Ismaili teaching as a young man and became a da'i. He studied in Isfahan and Cairo, then returned to Persia and seized Alamut in 1090. After al-Mustansir's death he supported Nizar's line and built an independent Nizari network of fortresses and communities. He died at Alamut in 1124. The familiar 'Assassin' legends about drugged followers are not supported by reliable historical evidence.

Leader of the Syrian Nizari Ismailis

Rashid al-Din Sinan

d. 1193 CE · active in Syria c. 1160s–1193

Born near Basra into a Shi'i family, Sinan grew up in Basra and became a schoolteacher before adopting Ismailism. He travelled to Alamut and studied under Hasan II, then was sent to Syria, where he became the leading Nizari da'i. From Masyaf and other strongholds he navigated conflicts with Crusader and Muslim powers. Western sources later called him the 'Old Man of the Mountain'; medieval Persian sources applied that phrase differently.

Chief judge and Ismaili jurist

Al-Qadi al-Nu'man

c. 903–974 CE

Born into a learned family in Qayrawan, al-Nu'man entered Fatimid service in 925 under al-Mahdi. He served the first four Fatimid Imam-caliphs as a judge, librarian and legal scholar, becoming chief judge in 948. Al-Mu'izz also entrusted him with teaching sessions at court. His Da'a'im al-Islam became a foundational work of Fatimid Ismaili law. He accompanied al-Mu'izz to Cairo and died there in 974.

Diplomat and Pakistan's UN representative

Prince Aly Khan

1911–1960 CE

Born in Turin, Italy, Aly Khan was the elder son of Aga Khan III. After a varied early life in Europe and South Asia, he entered diplomacy for Pakistan. In 1958 he became Pakistan's Permanent Representative to the United Nations, served as vice-president of the General Assembly and as vice-chair of the UN Peace Observation Commission. He died in a car accident near Paris in 1960.

UN High Commissioner for Refugees

Prince Sadruddin Aga Khan

1933–2003 CE · UNHCR head 1966–1977

Born in France, Sadruddin studied at Harvard and joined international service through UNESCO before working with UNHCR. He served as Deputy High Commissioner and, from 1966 to 1977, as High Commissioner—the youngest person to lead the agency at the time. His tenure responded to major refugee crises in Africa and Asia. Later he worked on UN assistance missions and environmental protection through the Bellerive Foundation. He died in Boston in 2003.

AKDN board leader and arts patron

Prince Amyn Aga Khan

born 1937 · current

Born in Geneva in 1937, Amyn is the younger brother of Aga Khan IV and was educated at Le Rosey and Harvard. He pursued a career in economics and finance before taking senior governance roles across Aga Khan Development Network agencies. He chairs or serves on boards connected to the Aga Khan Foundation, AKFED, the Aga Khan Agency for Microfinance and the Aga Khan Trust for Culture, and has helped shape the Aga Khan Museum and cultural programmes.

Wildlife photographer and conservationist

Prince Hussain Aga Khan

born 1974 · current

Hussain Aga Khan is a son of Aga Khan IV and an internationally exhibited wildlife photographer. His work documents marine life and threatened ecosystems, using photography to encourage conservation. He founded Focused on Nature and has partnered with the Aga Khan Development Network, UNEP and museums on exhibitions and public education. Public biographies focus on his conservation work rather than private childhood details.

Historian of Ismaili studies

Farhad Daftary

born 1938 · Director Emeritus, IIS

Born in Tehran, Daftary studied in Iran, Europe and the United States and earned a PhD in economics from the University of California, Berkeley. Since joining the Institute of Ismaili Studies in 1988, he led its research department and served as co-director until 2023; he is now Director Emeritus and a governor. He has written more than 200 articles and reference entries and authored major books on Ismaili history, including The Ismailis: Their History and Doctrines and The Ismaili Imams.

Harvard scholar of Ismaili and South Asian Muslim traditions

Ali S. Asani

born and raised in Nairobi · Harvard faculty since 1983

Born and raised in Nairobi, Asani moved to the United States for Harvard College and graduated summa cum laude in 1977. He has taught at Harvard since 1983 and became a leading scholar of Ismaili and Sufi devotional traditions in South Asia. His books study ginans, Indic-language Ismaili literature, devotional poetry and religious literacy. He has served in senior academic roles at Harvard and on its Pluralism Project and Religious Literacy Project.

Scholar of media, Muslims and diaspora

Karim H. Karim

career documented; personal birth details not published

Karim is a Chancellor’s Professor at Carleton University and directs its Centre for the Study of Islam. His research examines media representations of Muslims, diaspora and communication. He previously directed Carleton’s School of Journalism and Communication and co-directed the Institute of Ismaili Studies from 2009 to 2011. He has also advised the development of media and communication programmes at the Aga Khan University and University of Central Asia.

Scholar and former Director of the Institute of Ismaili Studies

Azim Nanji

career documented; personal birth details not published

Nanji is a scholar of religion, Islam and Ismailism who served as Director of the Institute of Ismaili Studies from 1998 to 2008. He later held senior appointments at Stanford and advised the Provost at Aga Khan University. His work includes The Nizari Ismaili Tradition, Mapping Islamic Studies and The Historical Atlas of Islam. Across his career he has combined research, university leadership and public education on religious diversity.
