From mudbrick to stone

The earliest city walls were commissioned by Jawhar al-Siqilli under al-Muʿizz when Cairo was founded in 969. In 1087, the Fatimid vizier Badr al-Jamali commissioned the expansion and rebuilding of the walls in stone. The surviving northern and southern gates belong to this wider history.

Later extensions

The Ayyubid and Mamluk periods substantially altered the wider defensive and urban landscape. The UNESCO record distinguishes Fatimid planning from subsequent extensions; the later Cairo Citadel should not be mislabelled a Fatimid structure.
