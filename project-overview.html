The problem and the response

Scattered historical material

Historical material is distributed across books, academic publications, institutional records and popular accounts. Readers need clear routes to sources and a distinction between evidence, interpretation and tradition.

Connected research records

HSKRC connects library records, biographies, articles and heritage documentation in an English–Persian environment. It offers a starting point for research; each claim remains open to correction as stronger evidence emerges.

Evidence a reviewer can examine

Historical research

The Alamut guide includes claim-level references, a bibliography and a dated editorial revision. Independent scholarly peer review has not yet been recorded.

Community heritage documentation

The Lakhshom Qala profile attributes local testimony and identifies gaps for future fieldwork.

Editorial accountability

Source records, editorial rules and the public contact route allow readers to question claims. External scholars are not presented as project partners.

Visits and page views measure website activity, not learning gains or conservation outcomes. Next priorities are independent subject review, contributor documentation with consent and evidence of how readers use the archive.

Responsibility and research standards

Founded and developed by Kamal Barlas, HSKRC is an independent project. Inclusion of external scholars, institutions or sources does not imply affiliation or endorsement. AI-assisted work is not treated as a scholarly source.
