Alamut

A research node on Alamut Castle, its landscape, its role in the Nizari Ismaili state, its connection with Hassan Sabbah, and its recognition as part of the 2026 World Heritage property.

Alborz MountainsQazvin ProvinceWorld Heritage · 2026
Overview

Alamut as a historical centre

The fortress and the state

Encyclopaedia Iranica identifies Alamut as a mountain fortress in the central Alborz, in the Rudbar region. Hassan Sabbah seized the castle in 1090 and made it the centre of the Ismaili movement. UNESCO's 2026 World Heritage decision describes Alamut Castle as the headquarters of the Nizari Ismaili state founded by Hassan Sabbah in 1090.

Landscape & Engineering

A fortress shaped by its landscape

Iranica describes Alamut as standing at about 2,000 metres on a rocky crest reached through a narrow approach. The fortified sites of the Alamut and Rudbar network were designed to withstand long sieges, with storage capacity and water systems including cisterns, qanats and canals. Hassan Sabbah also improved cultivation and irrigation in the Alamut valley to strengthen food self-sufficiency. [HSKRC-SRC-0002]

Historical Timeline

Key milestones

Before 1090

A fortified site in Daylam

Iranica notes earlier associations of the fortress with the Daylam region and local rulers before its capture by the Ismailis.

1090

Hassan Sabbah captures Alamut

Hassan Sabbah seized the fortress and made it the centre of the Ismaili movement. Iranica says this effectively marked the foundation of the Nizari Ismaili state in Persia. [HSKRC-SRC-0001] [HSKRC-SRC-0008]

1092

Saljuq counterattack repulsed

Iranica records a Saljuq counterattack in 485/1092 and a period in which the Ismailis consolidated their new state.

1109–1118

Long Saljuq pressure

Iranica describes a prolonged campaign against Alamut begun in 503/1109. The fortress successfully resisted the attacks until the death of Sultan Muhammad Tapar in 1118.

1124

Death of Hassan Sabbah

Hassan Sabbah died in 518/1124 and was buried near Alamut, according to Encyclopaedia Iranica.

1256

Mongol destruction

Iranica records the destruction of Alamut by the Mongols in 654/1256. UNESCO's World Heritage documentation preserves the site's wider historical significance beyond this event.

2026

World Heritage inscription

The World Heritage Committee inscribed Alamūt Castle and Related Fortifications on the World Heritage List under criterion (iii).

World Heritage

Alamūt Castle and Related Fortifications

In 2026, the World Heritage Committee inscribed the serial property “Alamūt Castle and Related Fortifications” in Iran on the World Heritage List under criterion (iii). UNESCO's decision describes seven castles and fortifications distributed across the Alamūt Valley in Qazvin Province, with Alamut Castle at the centre and the other six sites forming a coordinated defensive system.
