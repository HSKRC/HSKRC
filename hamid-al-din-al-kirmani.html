Life: from origins to final years

A chronological biography based on the best available historical evidence.

Unknown date

Probable Persian origin

The exact date of Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani's birth is unknown. His nisba, al-Kirmani, and later evidence connect him with the Persian province of Kirman, and scholars generally regard him as Persian in origin. As with several senior Ismaili missionaries, secrecy surrounding da'wa activity means that no reliable childhood narrative has survived.

Early career

Rise within the Ismaili da'wa

By the time he becomes visible in the historical record, al-Kirmani was already a highly educated Ismaili da'i with command of theology, philosophy and the scientific learning of his age. He spent a large part of his career in Iraq, especially in environments connected with Baghdad and Basra, while also maintaining links with central and western Iran.

Iraq and Iran

Chief missionary activity

Later Ismaili tradition associates him with the title Hujjat al-'Iraqayn, suggesting authority across both Arab Iraq and the Iranian region historically called 'Iraq al-'Ajam. His mission targeted not only ordinary followers but influential rulers and tribal leaders whose political support could challenge Abbasid power. This brought the Fatimid da'wa into direct competition with the Abbasid caliphate.

1011

Abbasid reaction and the Baghdad Manifesto

The success of Fatimid-Ismaili activity in Iraq alarmed the Abbasid caliph al-Qadir. In 1011 he sponsored the so-called Baghdad Manifesto, a public anti-Fatimid statement challenging Fatimid claims of descent from the Prophet's family. Al-Kirmani's career belongs directly to this atmosphere of intense religious, political and propaganda conflict.

Early 11th century

Summoned to Cairo

By the final years of the reign of Imam-Caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah, the central Fatimid da'wa in Cairo regarded al-Kirmani as one of its most learned theologians. He was summoned to Cairo to help address serious doctrinal disputes that threatened the unity of the da'wa. During this period he wrote treatises defending the doctrine of the Imamate and clarifying Ismaili teaching.

Mature thought

Rahat al-'Aql and systematic philosophy

Al-Kirmani's mature writings brought together metaphysics, cosmology, theology and political-religious authority in an unusually systematic form. Rahat al-'Aql, generally regarded as his major work, presents a highly structured vision of intellect, creation and spiritual hierarchy. His thought also shows engagement with broader philosophical traditions of the Islamic world rather than isolation from them.

After 1020

Final years and uncertain death

The surviving evidence places al-Kirmani alive into the early eleventh century, and modern scholarship commonly describes his death as occurring after 1020. The exact date and place are not known. Because he does not appear clearly in contemporary narrative chronicles, much of his biography has to be reconstructed from his own writings and later Ismaili sources.

Al-Kirmani's intellectual career is well documented through his writings; his birth, childhood and exact death are not.

Overview

Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani was one of the most learned Ismaili da'is and philosopher-theologians of the Fatimid period. He flourished during the reign of the Fatimid imam-caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah and produced a substantial body of doctrinal and philosophical writing.

Career and da'wa

Biographical information about al-Kirmani is limited, partly because senior da'is often worked in politically sensitive environments. Modern studies indicate that he was already a leading figure in the Ismaili da'wa in Iraq and Iran before being summoned to Cairo, where he participated in important doctrinal debates during the later years of al-Hakim's reign.

Philosophy and theology

Al-Kirmani brought together theology, philosophy and cosmology in a highly systematic way. Scholarship on his works notes his engagement with philosophical traditions associated with al-Farabi, Neoplatonism and astronomical models. He reworked these materials within an Ismaili theological framework rather than simply reproducing them.

Major works

  • Rahat al-'Aql — his best-known systematic philosophical work.
  • Kitab al-Riyad — a work engaging doctrinal questions and earlier Ismaili authorities.
  • Numerous shorter treatises and epistles linked to debates of the Fatimid period.

Legacy

Al-Kirmani is central to the study of mature Fatimid Ismaili philosophy. His writings show the extent to which the Fatimid intellectual world participated in the broader philosophical culture of medieval Islam while maintaining its own distinctive doctrinal concerns.
