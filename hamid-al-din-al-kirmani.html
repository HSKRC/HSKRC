Overview

Hamid al-Din al-Kirmani was one of the most learned Ismaili da'is and philosopher-theologians of the Fatimid period. He flourished during the reign of the Fatimid imam-caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah and produced a substantial body of doctrinal and philosophical writing.

Career and da'wa

Biographical information about al-Kirmani is limited, partly because senior da'is often worked in politically sensitive environments. Modern studies indicate that he was already a leading figure in the Ismaili da'wa in Iraq and Iran before being summoned to Cairo, where he participated in important doctrinal debates during the later years of al-Hakim's reign.

Philosophy and theology

Al-Kirmani brought together theology, philosophy and cosmology in a highly systematic way. Scholarship on his works notes his engagement with philosophical traditions associated with al-Farabi, Neoplatonism and astronomical models. He reworked these materials within an Ismaili theological framework rather than simply reproducing them.

Major works

  • Rahat al-'Aql — his best-known systematic philosophical work.
  • Kitab al-Riyad — a work engaging doctrinal questions and earlier Ismaili authorities.
  • Numerous shorter treatises and epistles linked to debates of the Fatimid period.

Legacy

Al-Kirmani is central to the study of mature Fatimid Ismaili philosophy. His writings show the extent to which the Fatimid intellectual world participated in the broader philosophical culture of medieval Islam while maintaining its own distinctive doctrinal concerns.
