Historical context

A connected chapter of Ismaili history

The Fatimid caliphate began in North Africa in 909 CE under the Ismaili imam-caliph ʿAbd Allah al-Mahdi. Its rulers later established a new capital in Egypt. The legacy of the era includes urban planning, institutions of learning, libraries, religious scholarship and architecture.

For HSKRC, the Fatimid era is the historical background to the later Nizari Ismaili tradition. Following the contested succession in 1094, the Nizari and Mustaʿli branches developed separately; the earlier Fatimid legacy remains part of their shared history.

Chronology

Milestones from 909 to 1171

  1. 909

    Foundation of the Fatimid state

    The Ismaili imam-caliph al-Mahdi founded the caliphate in North Africa.

  2. 969

    Cairo founded

    Jawhar al-Siqilli founded the new capital on the orders of al-Muʿizz.

  3. 970–972

    Al-Azhar Mosque built

    Construction began in 970; the mosque was inaugurated in 972. It subsequently grew into a major scholarly centre.

  4. 1005

    Dar al-Ilm established

    The imam-caliph al-Hakim established an institution of scholarship and a major library in Cairo.

  5. 1090

    Alamut becomes a Nizari-era centre

    Hassan Sabbah took control of Alamut; the Nizari state emerged from this movement.

  6. 1094

    Succession dispute

    After al-Mustansir’s death, disputes over the succession divided the Ismaili community.

  7. 1171

    End of Fatimid rule in Egypt

    Fatimid rule ended under Salah al-Din, while its cultural and intellectual legacy endured.
