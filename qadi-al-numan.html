Life: from birth to death

A chronological biography based on the best available historical evidence.

c. 903

Birth in Qayrawan

Qadi al-Nu'man was born around 903 in Qayrawan in Ifriqiya, North Africa, into a learned family. The surviving record contains little detail about his childhood, but the Institute of Ismaili Studies notes that his father had apparently converted from Maliki Sunnism to Ismaili Shi'ism. Al-Nu'man was consequently educated within an Ismaili intellectual and religious environment.

925

Entry into Fatimid service

In 925, while still a relatively young man, al-Nu'man entered the service of the Fatimid Imam-Caliph al-Mahdi, founder of the Fatimid caliphate in North Africa. This began a career that would continue under the first four Fatimid Imam-Caliphs. His long service gave him unusual access to the development of Fatimid institutions, law and doctrine from within the state itself.

934–946

Service under al-Qa'im

After al-Mahdi's death, al-Nu'man continued to serve the Fatimid administration under al-Qa'im. The period was politically difficult and included major internal challenges to Fatimid rule. Although the surviving biographical record does not document every office he held year by year, his continued advancement indicates growing trust in his legal learning and administrative abilities.

948

Chief judge of the Fatimid state

Under Imam-Caliph al-Mansur, al-Nu'man served in roles that included keeper of the palace library and judge, including judicial service in Tripoli and Mansuriyya. In 948 al-Mansur appointed him chief judge of the Fatimid state. This appointment placed him at the centre of the developing Fatimid legal order.

954 onward

Authority under al-Mu'izz

The fourth Fatimid Imam-Caliph, al-Mu'izz, confirmed al-Nu'man as chief judge. In 954 he was also entrusted with mazalim, the hearing of grievances across the Fatimid realm. Al-Mu'izz further authorised him to conduct the weekly majalis al-hikma, 'sessions of wisdom', in the palace for instruction in Ismaili doctrine, esoteric interpretation and law. His role therefore joined public jurisprudence with advanced religious teaching.

Mature career

Writing the Fatimid legal tradition

During these decades al-Nu'man produced the works that made him the foundational jurist of Fatimid Ismailism. His Da'a'im al-Islam organised rules concerning worship, family life, transactions and public obligations in a coherent legal structure under the authority of the Imam. He also wrote historical and interpretive works, preserving material that is indispensable for reconstructing early Fatimid and Ismaili history.

973

Migration to Egypt

After the Fatimid conquest of Egypt and the establishment of Cairo as the new imperial centre, al-Nu'man accompanied Imam-Caliph al-Mu'izz from North Africa to Egypt in 973. By then he was an elderly statesman, jurist and scholar whose career had spanned almost half a century of Fatimid history.

27 March 974

Death in Cairo

Al-Nu'man died in Cairo on 27 March 974, the last day of Jumada II 363 AH. The importance of his position is reflected in the report that Imam-Caliph al-Mu'izz personally led his funeral prayer. His family continued to occupy important positions in Fatimid judicial and scholarly life, while his works remained authoritative in later Ismaili traditions.

The early childhood record is sparse; later offices and the date of death are much better documented.

Overview

Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, better known as Qadi al-Nu'man, was the foremost jurist of the early Fatimid state and one of the foundational authors of Ismaili legal and doctrinal literature. His long service under the first Fatimid imam-caliphs gave him a central role in shaping Fatimid jurisprudence.

Life and service

Modern scholarship places his birth around 903 in Qayrawan in North Africa. He entered Fatimid service in the tenth century and served the first four Fatimid imam-caliphs in a number of capacities. Sources cited by the Institute of Ismaili Studies note roles including palace librarian and judge, before he emerged as the leading judicial authority of the Fatimid realm.

Jurisprudence

Qadi al-Nu'man systematised legal doctrine for the Fatimid Ismaili community. His jurisprudence brought together ritual, family, commercial and public-law questions within a coherent framework tied to the authority of the Imam. His legal writings became especially important in later Ismaili traditions.

Major works

  • Da'a'im al-Islam — his best-known legal work and a foundational text of Fatimid Ismaili jurisprudence.
  • Iftitah al-Da'wa — an important historical account of the beginnings of the Fatimid da'wa and state.
  • Asas al-Ta'wil — associated with esoteric interpretation of scripture and religious history.

Historical importance

His writings are indispensable sources for the study of Fatimid institutions, Ismaili law and early Ismaili intellectual history. Because he wrote from within the Fatimid establishment, his works also preserve valuable information about the formation and self-understanding of the Fatimid state.
