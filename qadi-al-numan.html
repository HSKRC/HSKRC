Overview

Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man ibn Muhammad, better known as Qadi al-Nu'man, was the foremost jurist of the early Fatimid state and one of the foundational authors of Ismaili legal and doctrinal literature. His long service under the first Fatimid imam-caliphs gave him a central role in shaping Fatimid jurisprudence.

Life and service

Modern scholarship places his birth around 903 in Qayrawan in North Africa. He entered Fatimid service in the tenth century and served the first four Fatimid imam-caliphs in a number of capacities. Sources cited by the Institute of Ismaili Studies note roles including palace librarian and judge, before he emerged as the leading judicial authority of the Fatimid realm.

Jurisprudence

Qadi al-Nu'man systematised legal doctrine for the Fatimid Ismaili community. His jurisprudence brought together ritual, family, commercial and public-law questions within a coherent framework tied to the authority of the Imam. His legal writings became especially important in later Ismaili traditions.

Major works

  • Da'a'im al-Islam — his best-known legal work and a foundational text of Fatimid Ismaili jurisprudence.
  • Iftitah al-Da'wa — an important historical account of the beginnings of the Fatimid da'wa and state.
  • Asas al-Ta'wil — associated with esoteric interpretation of scripture and religious history.

Historical importance

His writings are indispensable sources for the study of Fatimid institutions, Ismaili law and early Ismaili intellectual history. Because he wrote from within the Fatimid establishment, his works also preserve valuable information about the formation and self-understanding of the Fatimid state.
