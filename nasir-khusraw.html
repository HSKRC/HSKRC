Overview

Nasir Khusraw was one of the most important Persian-speaking Ismaili thinkers of the Fatimid period. He combined poetry, philosophy, religious teaching and travel writing, and his works later became especially influential among Ismaili communities in Iran, Afghanistan and Central Asia.

Life and journey

Born in 1004 in the wider region of Khurasan, Nasir Khusraw initially moved within courtly and administrative circles. According to the biographical tradition preserved around his own writings, a period of spiritual questioning led him to undertake a long journey that included the Hijaz and Fatimid Cairo. He spent several years in Cairo, then a major intellectual and political centre of the Fatimid world.

Ismaili role

The Institute of Ismaili Studies describes Nasir Khusraw as having converted to Ismaili Shi'ism and later being appointed hujjat for Khurasan. In Ismaili usage, this title indicated a senior position within the da'wa hierarchy. After his return east, opposition and persecution contributed to his withdrawal to Badakhshan, where he composed much of his surviving work.

Thought and intellectual contribution

His writings explore the relationship between reason, revelation, cosmology, ethics and religious interpretation. He argued that knowledge and disciplined understanding were central to the religious life, and he expressed complex philosophical ideas in both Persian prose and poetry. His work is important for studying the intellectual history of medieval Ismailism as well as Persian literature.

Major works

  • Safarnama — account of his travels.
  • Wajh-i Din — a major work on religious interpretation.
  • Gushayish wa Rahayish — theological and philosophical questions and answers.
  • Jami' al-Hikmatayn — an important philosophical work.
  • His Diwan — Persian poetry with religious, ethical and philosophical themes.

Legacy

Nasir Khusraw remains a major figure of Persian literature and Ismaili intellectual history. In Badakhshan and neighbouring regions his memory acquired enduring religious and cultural importance. Modern scholarship studies him simultaneously as a poet, philosopher, traveller and representative of the Fatimid-Ismaili intellectual tradition.
