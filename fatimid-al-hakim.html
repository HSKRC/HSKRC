The Mosque of al-Hakim in Cairo

Foundation and completion

The large congregational mosque known as al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah was begun under the Fatimid imam-caliph al-ʿAziz around 990 CE and completed under his son al-Hakim in 1013. Egypt’s Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities records the site’s location near Bab al-Futuh at the northern end of al-Muʿizz Street.

Plan and architectural character

The mosque includes a broad courtyard bordered by arcades and a monumental projecting entrance. Two minarets flank the main frontage. The site was at one time outside the earliest Fatimid wall, later enclosed by the enlarged fortifications. Its present appearance incorporates the results of successive restorations.
