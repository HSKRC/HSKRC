The Nizari Ismaili Imams

Biographies of the Imams in the Nizari Ismaili line, from Ali ibn Abi Talib to the present Imam, Mawlana Shah Rahim al-Hussaini, Aga Khan V.

This page follows the 49-Imam biographical sequence published by the Institute of Ismaili Studies through Aga Khan IV, and adds the current 50th Imam using the official Ismaili community biography. Historical records are uneven: dates and personal details are uncertain or undocumented for many early and concealed Imams. Where evidence is sparse, this is stated rather than filled with invented details. Honorifics are shown after every Imam’s name as requested.

Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.)

c. 600–661 CE

The Ismaili Imamate traces its hereditary line to Ali, cousin and son-in-law of Prophet Muhammad and husband of Fatima. He became the fourth caliph in 656 and was killed in Kufa in 661; Ismaili sources honour him as the first Imam.

Husayn ibn Ali (a.s.)

626–680 CE

Husayn, grandson of Prophet Muhammad and son of Ali and Fatima, became a central figure in early Muslim history. He was killed with members of his family and companions at Karbala in 680, an event commemorated across Shi'i communities.

Ali Zayn al-Abidin (a.s.)

c. 659–713 CE

The son of Husayn, Ali Zayn al-Abidin survived Karbala and became the next Imam. Later Muslim tradition remembers his devotion, teaching and prayers, including the collection known as al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya.

'Abd Allah al-Mahdi Billah (a.s.)

873–934 CE · Imam-Caliph from 909

Born Sa'id ibn al-Husayn in Salamiyya, he led the Ismaili da'wa while its leadership was concealed and travelled west as Abbasid pressure grew. In 909 he was proclaimed Imam and caliph in Ifriqiya, founding the Fatimid state; he established a new capital at Mahdiyya.

al-Mustansir Billah (a.s.)

1029–1094 CE · Imam-Caliph 1036–1094

Al-Mustansir became Imam-Caliph as a child and ruled for nearly six decades, one of the longest reigns in Islamic history. Political upheaval and famine weakened the state; after his death, a succession dispute between his sons Nizar and al-Musta'li led to the Nizari and Musta'li branches.

Nizar ibn al-Mustansir (a.s.)

c. 1045–1095 CE · dates approximate

Nizar was the eldest son of al-Mustansir and, according to Nizari accounts, had been designated to succeed him. After the vizier al-Afdal installed al-Musta'li, Nizar's claim was rejected; his revolt failed and he died in captivity, while his supporters maintained his line of Imamat.

Shah Karim al-Husayni (Aga Khan IV) (a.s.)

1936–2025 CE · Imam 1957–2025

Karim al-Husayni succeeded his grandfather in 1957 at age 20 and became the 49th Nizari Imam. He founded the Aga Khan Development Network in 1967, guiding its work in health, education, culture and economic development; he died in Lisbon in February 2025.

Shah Rahim al-Hussaini (Aga Khan V) (a.s.)

born 12 October 1971 · 50th Imam since 4 February 2025

Born in Geneva, Rahim al-Hussaini succeeded his father, Aga Khan IV, on 4 February 2025 as the 50th hereditary Imam of the Nizari Ismailis. Before his succession he served in the Aga Khan Development Network and its institutions; he continues the Imamat today.
