A mosque from the later Fatimid age

The Fatimid caliph al-Amir bi-Ahkam Allah commissioned al-Aqmar Mosque in 1125 CE. Egypt’s Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities identifies the vizier al-Maʾmun al-Bataʾihi as supervisor of construction. The mosque stands on al-Muʿizz Street in Cairo.

Research collection

Carved facade and orientation

The sculpted stone facade is a notable example of Fatimid architectural ornament. Kufic inscriptions and the repeated names Muhammad and Ali are part of its program. The facade follows the street while the prayer space respects the qibla direction, demonstrating a deliberate architectural solution.

Research collection

Later interventions

The building underwent later repair and alteration, including Mamluk-period renovations. A modern photo must not be read as a perfect record of the 1125 fabric.
