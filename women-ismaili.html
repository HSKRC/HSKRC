A source-led biographical collection tracing women’s documented lives and contributions across the Ismaili world and its study, from the medieval period to today.

Historical classification: this collection includes women in the shared early and Fatimid Ismaili past, later Nizari communities, and scholars of Ismaili history. Inclusion is not a claim that every historical figure belonged to the Nizari branch. Succession after 1094 and the identities of other Ismaili traditions are distinguished where relevant.

Editorial note: the early historical record is uneven. For the six pre-modern women shown with artwork, no authenticated contemporary portrait is presented here; each image is an AI-assisted artistic interpretation, not documentary evidence. Modern portraits link to their original institutional or archival sources.

Fatimid Egypt · political leadership

Sitt al-Mulk

970–1023 CE

Sitt al-Mulk was born in 970 in the Fatimid palace, the daughter of al-Aziz bi’llah and al-Sayyida. Her title means “Lady of the Empire.” She became a prominent entrepreneur and wielded influence at court during her half-brother al-Hakim’s reign. When he disappeared in 1021, she secured the accession of his son al-Zahir and acted as regent, exercising de facto authority during the early years of his rule. Her career unfolded amid competing court factions and disputes over succession, so chroniclers’ accounts of her actions require careful comparison. She died in 1023, leaving the Fatimid state under al-Zahir.

Yemen and Gujarat · ruler and hujja

Queen Arwa al-Sulayhi

c. 1048–1138 CE

Arwa al-Sulayhi, born in Haraz around 1048, was orphaned young and raised at the Sulayhid court by her relatives Ali al-Sulayhi and Asma bint Shihab. Her education prepared her for public leadership. She married Ahmad al-Mukarram in 1066 and became politically active as his health declined; after his death in 1084 she governed first as regent for their son, then as sovereign in her own right from about 1097 until her death in 1138. The Fatimid Imam-Caliph al-Mustansir appointed her hujja of Yemen, giving her high religious authority as well as political leadership. She also directed the Ismaili mission in Yemen and western India.

Iran and India · community leadership

Bibi Sarkara Maryam Khatun (Mata Salamat)

18th–19th century

The Institute of Ismaili Studies identifies Maryam Khatun as the wife of Imam Shah Khalil Allah and mother of Hasan Ali Shah, later Aga Khan I; it does not provide a firm birth year. A community-published Ismaili historical text gives her birthplace as Iran and birth year as 1744, a detail not confirmed in the IIS biography. After her husband was killed in 1817, she sought justice from the Qajar court and helped safeguard her son’s succession. Later she travelled to India as Pir Mata Salamat, preached and helped settle community disputes. The IIS article’s body gives her death as Kutch in 1851, while its heading differs, so her death date is disputed.

South Asia · devotional literature

Sayyida Imam Begum

d. c. 1866 CE

Sayyida Imam Begum is remembered as a female composer of ginans, devotional poems in the South Asian Ismaili tradition. Published sources reviewed do not securely establish her birth, family, education or a complete life chronology. Her compositions survive within a tradition transmitted through performance and community memory, so individual attributions and biographical details require care. The Institute of Ismaili Studies recognizes her as one of the best-known women associated with ginan composition. This profile records that literary and devotional contribution while leaving unknown personal details marked as unknown rather than supplying an unverified life story.

South Asia and East Africa · community leadership

Begum Um Habibeh (Yvonne Labrousse)

1906–2000

Born Yvonne Blanche Labrousse in Sète, France, in 1906, she preferred the name Yvette. Before marriage she worked as a model and won a national beauty title, including Miss France in 1930, then travelled widely and lived in Egypt, where she embraced Islam. She married Imam Sultan Mahomed Shah Aga Khan III in Switzerland in 1944 and became Begum Um Habibeh. After his death in 1957 she continued charitable and community work, later establishing a retirement home in Le Cannet, France. She died on 1 July 2000 and was buried beside her husband in Aswan, Egypt.

Afghanistan · politics and women's rights

Farkhunda Zahra Naderi

born 1981

Farkhunda Zahra Naderi was born in 1981 in Kayan, Baghlan, into a family active in Afghan Ismaili community life. She began her education in Afghanistan, completed secondary studies in the United Kingdom and earned a law degree from Westminster International University in Tashkent. Elected to Afghanistan’s parliament in 2010, she became president of the Inter-Parliamentary Union’s Committee on Democracy and Human Rights in 2014—the first Afghan MP elected to one of its standing committees. She has spoken about human rights, women’s political participation and the choice to engage directly in public service. After the political transition in 2021, she served on Afghanistan’s High Council for National Reconciliation until 2022.

Global · institutional and social development

Princess Zahra Aga Khan

contemporary

Princess Zahra Aga Khan, born in 1970, is the eldest child of the late Aga Khan IV. She attended Le Rosey in Switzerland and graduated cum laude from Harvard in 1994 with a degree in Development Studies. For more than three decades she has held policy and management responsibilities in the Aga Khan Development Network, especially in health, education, planning and building services, and has served on institutional boards. She joined the Aga Khan University Board of Trustees in 2003 and was appointed Pro-Chancellor in 2025. Her work has connected long-term development policy with the governance and growth of AKU and other AKDN institutions.

Canada and Eritrea · diplomacy and gender equality

Nahla Valji

contemporary

Public biographies do not give Valji’s birth or childhood details. She holds a master’s degree in Political Science and International Relations and a joint diploma in Forced Migration Studies from York University. Her career began in South Africa, including work on human rights and gender-responsive budgeting and transitional justice; she helped found and edit the International Journal of Transitional Justice. Women of the Fatimid and Sulayhid worlds

Fatimid Egypt · regency

Rasad, mother of al-Mustansir

active c. 1036–c. 1070s

Rasad’s birth date and early life are not securely documented. Medieval accounts describe her as a woman of Nubian or Sudanese origin who entered the Fatimid palace and became the mother of al-Mustansir. When her son succeeded to the throne as a young child in 1036, she served as regent and came to dominate political affairs for decades; by the mid-1040s she was publicly styled queen in state documents. Her authority later diminished as military factions and powerful viziers gained control, though she remained an influential queen mother. The surviving evidence is filtered through court chronicles, and her precise death date remains uncertain.

Yemen · statecraft and education

Asma bint Shihab

d. 1074 CE

Asma bint Shihab was the cousin and wife of Ali al-Sulayhi, founder of Yemen’s Sulayhid dynasty. She took an active part in state affairs and helped educate Arwa, the future queen, at the Sulayhid court. After Ali was killed by the Najahids in 1067, their son Ahmad al-Mukarram succeeded him with Fatimid investiture; Asma supported her son’s government, as she had supported her husband’s rule. Her political activity was unusual in its setting and is preserved mainly in Ismaili historical accounts. She died in 1074, after which Arwa became the effective ruler of Sulayhid Yemen.

Education and community building in South Asia

Archival photo c. 1963; image credit: Ismaili Special Collections Unit.
Punjab · girls’ education

Vazir Saheba Hajjan Ghulam Fatimah

1889–1967

Born Sobha Devi in Amritsar in 1889, Vazir Saheba Hajjan Ghulam Fatimah later became known for her work in Gujranwala, Punjab. According to an IIS oral-history account based on family testimony, she left secure employment as a midwife and began teaching girls in a family courtyard with Saleha Bibi. The two women canvassed local households, taught without fees and organized a women-led association to acquire land for a permanent school. The Chaudhry Fateh Din Girls’ Islamia High School later expanded into branches and continued serving generations of students. Fatimah also taught Qur’an reading to neighbourhood girls and remained active in community life; the oral-history source preserves both the achievements and limits of the family record.

Gujranwala · community education

Saleha Bibi

dates not established

Saleha Bibi is known through an IIS oral-history project as the educational partner of Vazir Saheba Ghulam Fatimah in Gujranwala. The family account recalls that the two women began teaching girls on rugs in a courtyard because the area lacked a Muslim school for girls. They visited households to encourage enrolment, charged no fees and raised money for supplies; they then organized women collectively to secure land and establish a permanent school. The Chaudhry Fateh Din Girls’ Islamia High School later developed branches and continued its educational work. Published sources offer few independent biographical details about Saleha’s childhood or later life, so her documented educational contribution is clearer than her private biography.

Women who document Ismaili heritage

These nine scholars are included for documented research on Ismaili and Shi'i history and communities. The IIS profiles do not establish all their religious identities; this section records their scholarship and does not infer affiliation.

Photo: Institute of Ismaili Studies.
Lecturer, historian and author; IIS governor

Dr Nadia Eboo-Jamal

documented career

The sources reviewed do not give Nadia Eboo-Jamal’s birth or childhood details. She earned a PhD in Near Eastern Languages and Literature from New York University in 1996 and became a historian of Persian history and culture under Mongol rule, especially the Nizari Ismailis. Her book Surviving the Mongols (2002) follows the community’s networks and continuity through the Mongol period. She has taught and supported religious education in the United Kingdom and United States, contributed to IIS curriculum materials, and serves on the IIS Board of Governors.

Dr Shainool Jiwa

documented career

Public biographies do not state Jiwa’s birth or childhood. Her education is documented: BA in Political Science and Philosophy at the University of Karachi (1979), MLitt at Edinburgh (1981), an MA through the IIS–McGill programme (1984), and a PhD in Islamic Studies at Edinburgh (1989); she later completed counselling and academic-practice qualifications. Dr Tazim Kassam

documented career

No reliable birth or childhood account was located. Kassam earned a diploma in Hindustani classical vocal music at the University of Bombay (1982), a BA in Philosophy and Religion at McGill (1984), and a PhD in History of Religions at McGill (1993). Scholar of Islam, gender and religious studies

Emerita Professor Zayn Kassam

documented career

Born and raised in Kenya, Zayn Kassam attended Aga Khan primary schools in Kisumu, Mombasa and Nairobi, then Aga Khan High School in Nairobi, graduating in 1972. She moved to Canada for university and earned a BA in Religious Studies, an MA in Islamic Studies and a PhD in History of Religions at McGill, specialising in medieval Islamic and Indian philosophy. She taught at Pomona College for many years, where she also held leadership roles and won teaching awards. She is Director of the Institute of Ismaili Studies and Head of its Department of Academic Research; her writing also covers gender, environment, migration and religion.

Gender and Muslim societies scholar; Georgetown University

Dr Shenila Khoja-Moolji

documented career

Khoja-Moolji grew up in Hyderabad, Pakistan. In her autobiographical account, she recalls that the 1971 war displaced her family to Karachi, where as a child she helped make bhel puri for a canteen near the local jamatkhana. She later pursued graduate study in education and feminist theory and became a scholar of Muslim communities, gender, race, displacement and power. She holds the Hamad bin Khalifa al-Thani Endowed Chair of Muslim Societies at Georgetown University. Her books explore girls’ education, political belonging and displaced Shia Ismaili women; she has also served Ismaili community councils and educational programmes.

Historian of early Shi'i thought and medieval Arabic

Dr Arzina Lalani

documented career

Lalani earned a doctorate in Arabic and Middle Eastern Studies at the University of Edinburgh and served as a researcher, writer and lecturer at the Institute of Ismaili Studies. Her work examines early Shi‘i thought and the formation of Imami doctrines, including the teachings of Imam Muhammad al-Baqir. Her award-winning Early Shi‘i Thought (2000) has been translated into several languages. She later edited and translated Aḥmad al-Naysābūrī’s Fatimid treatise Kitāb ithbāt al-imāma as Degrees of Excellence (2009), alongside publishing scholarly articles. The sources reviewed do not give her birth or family details.

Dr Gurdofarid Miskinzoda

documented career

Miskinzoda completed a BA with distinction (1998) and MA with distinction (2000) in Arabic and Persian studies at Moscow State University, a PhD at SOAS in 2007, and an MBA in Higher Education Management at UCL in 2019. She has taught since 2004 and joined IIS in 2007, where she held research, scholarship and Shi‘i Studies coordination roles. She teaches on IIS graduate programmes and is Managing Editor of its Shi‘i Heritage Series. Her research spans the history of Islam to the seventeenth century, Arabic and Persian literature, and Shi‘i historical traditions; she co-edited The Study of Shi‘i Islam (2014).

Scholar of Khojki manuscripts and South Asian Ismaili traditions

Zawahir Moir

documented career

Born Zawahir Noorally in Karachi in 1933, she earned a BA in Arabic and Islamic Culture (1956) and a master’s in Islamic History (1958) at Karachi University. An Aga Khan scholarship took her to SOAS in London for a further MA. She returned to Karachi and worked with the Ismailia Association for Pakistan for nearly 15 years, then moved back to London in 1979. At IIS she catalogued Khojki manuscripts and continued teaching the script after leaving. A pioneer in Satpanth and ginan studies, she co-authored Ismaili Hymns from South Asia (2001). She died in London on 9 February 2024, aged 91.

Uganda and Canada · girls’ education and public service

Senator Farah Mohamed

born in Kampala; appointed senator 2025

Born in Kampala, Uganda, Mohamed came to Canada as a refugee child after the 1972 expulsion of Asians from Uganda and grew up there. She studied at Queen’s University and the University of Western Ontario, then worked in public service, philanthropy and youth development. In 2010 she founded G(irls)20, later led the Malala Fund and The King’s Trust Canada, and was appointed to the Canadian Senate on 7 March 2025. The Ismaili community profile describes her upbringing in the Ismaili Muslim faith.

Zahira Virani

UN development leader · current role in Central African Republic

The public biographies reviewed focus on Virani’s education and career and do not give her birth date or childhood. She served as Resident Coordinator in São Tomé and Príncipe and Angola, then moved to the UNDP Resident Representative role in the Central African Republic in 2025. The IIS identifies her as an alumna in its Ismaili studies programme.

Uganda and Canada · public service and community leadership

Salma Lakhani

born in Kampala · Lieutenant Governor since 2020

Born and raised in Kampala, Lakhani grew up in an Ismaili Muslim community and studied at Aga Khan School before completing an honours degree in clinical biochemistry at the University of Manchester. During Uganda’s 1972 expulsion of Asians she became stateless; she later settled in Edmonton with her husband in 1977, became a Canadian citizen and built a career in early-childhood education and volunteer service. She served many Alberta community organisations and became the province’s 19th Lieutenant Governor on 26 August 2020; she remains in office in the current official biography.
