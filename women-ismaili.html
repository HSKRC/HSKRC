Fatimid Egypt · political leadership

Sitt al-Mulk

970–1023 CE

Born in 970 in the Fatimid palace, she was the daughter of Imam-Caliph al-Aziz and al-Sayyida. She became an influential court figure and entrepreneur. After her half-brother al-Hakim disappeared in 1021, she helped secure the succession of her nephew al-Zahir and governed as regent until her death on 5 February 1023.

Yemen and Gujarat · ruler and hujja

Queen Arwa al-Sulayhi

c. 1048–1138 CE

Born around 1048, Arwa was raised at the Sulayhid court and married Ahmad al-Mukarram. When illness weakened his rule, she took on state responsibilities; after his death in 1084 she became Yemen’s effective ruler. Imam-Caliph al-Mustansir appointed her hujja and entrusted her with the da'wa in Yemen and western India. She supported the Musta'li line after the 1094 succession crisis and governed from Dhu Jibla until her death in 1138.

Iran and India · community leadership

Bibi Sarkara Maryam Khatun (Mata Salamat)

18th–19th century

Sources identify Maryam Khatun as wife of Imam Shah Khalil Allah and mother of Hasan Ali Shah, later Aga Khan I, but do not establish her birth or childhood. After her husband was killed in 1817, she sought justice from the Qajar court and helped safeguard her son’s succession. Later she travelled to India as Pir Mata Salamat, preached and helped settle community disputes. The IIS article conflicts on her death date: its body says Kutch, 1851, so the date is marked disputed here.

South Asia · devotional literature

Sayyida Imam Begum

d. c. 1866 CE

Published sources reviewed do not firmly establish Imam Begum’s birth, family or education. She is remembered as a South Asian Nizari Ismaili composer of ginans, devotional poems that transmit religious teaching through local languages and musical traditions. Her compositions continued in community recitation after her death, placed by IIS around 1866. The record preserves her literary and devotional work more clearly than her private life.

South Asia and East Africa · community leadership

Begum Um Habibeh (Yvonne Labrousse)

1906–2000

Yvonne Blanche Labrousse was born in Sète, France, in 1906 and was known as Yvette. She worked as a model and won Miss France in 1930; after travelling in Egypt she embraced Islam. She married Imam Sultan Mahomed Shah Aga Khan III in 1944 and became Begum Um Habibeh. She took a public role in Ismaili community affairs and encouraged women’s participation. She remained with her husband until his death in 1957 and died in southern France on 1 July 2000.

Afghanistan · politics and women's rights

Farkhunda Zahra Naderi

born 1981

Born in 1981 in Kayan, Baghlan, Naderi began school in Afghanistan and completed secondary education in the UK in 2001. She entered public life through political and civic work and was elected to Afghanistan’s parliament from Kabul in 2010. In 2013 she became the first Afghan MP elected to an IPU standing committee and in 2014 chaired its Committee on Democracy and Human Rights. She later served as a senior presidential adviser on UN affairs and on the High Council for National Reconciliation until August 2022; sources reviewed do not establish a later public appointment.

Global · institutional and social development

Princess Zahra Aga Khan

contemporary

Princess Zahra is the eldest daughter of Imam Aga Khan IV. She graduated from Harvard in 1994 with honours in Third World Development Studies and went on to work on social development institutions of the Ismaili Imamat and AKDN. Over more than two decades she took responsibilities across health and education and served on institutional boards. In February 2025 she became the first Pro-Chancellor of Aga Khan University, a role confirmed by the university’s current biography.

Canada and Eritrea · diplomacy and gender equality

Nahla Valji

contemporary

The UN biography documents Valji’s professional work rather than her birth or early family life. A Canadian specialist in gender equality, human rights, peace and security, and sustainable development, she built a career spanning more than 25 years across UN and civil-society work. In April 2024 the Secretary-General appointed her Resident Coordinator in Eritrea; UN reporting in 2025 describes her leading the country team there. The Ismaili community’s profile identifies her among Ismaili women serving at the United Nations.

Rasad, mother of al-Mustansir

active c. 1036–c. 1070s

Rasad’s birth date and early life are not recorded consistently. As the mother of the young Imam-Caliph al-Mustansir, she first acted as regent and later became a principal political authority at court. She influenced government through decades of shifting viziers, famine and civil conflict; the sources consulted do not establish a secure death date.

Yemen · statecraft and education

Asma bint Shihab

d. 1074 CE

Sources give little reliable detail about Asma’s birth or childhood. As wife of Sulayhid ruler Ali al-Sulayhi, she took part in state affairs and helped raise and educate her niece Arwa at court. After Ali was killed in 1067, Asma supported the rule of her son Ahmad al-Mukarram. She died in 1074; Arwa then assumed effective leadership of the Sulayhid state.

Punjab · girls’ education

Vazir Saheba Hajjan Ghulam Fatimah

1889–1967

Born Sobha Devi in Amritsar in 1889, she grew up in a Hindu household and later became known as Ghulam Fatimah in the Ismaili community. Widowed young, she pursued education and taught at a mission school in Gujranwala. With Saleha Bibi she began a girls’ school in a family courtyard, recruited pupils door to door and helped build a recognised high school with branches. She performed Hajj in 1954 and died on 26 August 1967.

Gujranwala · community education

Saleha Bibi

dates not established

The published oral history preserves only part of Saleha Bibi’s life. It names her as Ghulam Fatimah’s co-founder of the girls’ school in Gujranwala: they began teaching in a family courtyard, recruited pupils door to door and built a women-led educational initiative. The account says Saleha was buried beside Ghulam Fatimah at the school, but provides no birth, marriage or death dates. HSKRC records her documented work and leaves other life details open.

Dr Shainool Jiwa

The public profile focuses on her academic career, not her birth or childhood. Jiwa specialises in Fatimid history, teaches at IIS and edits its Living Ismaili Traditions and World of Islam series. Historian of early Shi'i thought and medieval Arabic

Dr Arzina Lalani

documented career

Lalani earned a doctorate in Arabic and Middle Eastern Studies at Edinburgh and worked in research, writing and teaching at IIS. Early Shi'i Thought (2000) examines Imam Muhammad al-Baqir and the formation of Shi'i doctrine. She later edited and translated the Fatimid treatise Degrees of Excellence (2009) and published further studies; the reviewed profiles give no birth or death dates.

Zawahir Moir

documented career

The IIS account gives no birth dates; her Ismaili scholarship began before the Institute’s 1977 founding. Uganda and Canada · girls’ education and public service

Senator Farah Mohamed

born in Kampala; appointed senator 2025

Born in Kampala, Uganda, Mohamed came to Canada as a refugee child after the 1972 expulsion of Asians from Uganda and grew up there. She studied at Queen’s University and the University of Western Ontario, then worked in public service, philanthropy and youth development. In 2010 she founded G(irls)20, later led the Malala Fund and The King’s Trust Canada, and was appointed to the Canadian Senate on 7 March 2025. The Ismaili community profile describes her upbringing in the Ismaili Muslim faith.

Canada and Africa · humanitarian and development service

Zahira Virani

UN development leader · current role in Central African Republic

The public biographies reviewed focus on Virani’s education and career and do not give her birth date or childhood. An IIS graduate (GPISH, 1997), she earned an MSc at the London School of Economics and has more than two decades of humanitarian and development experience. She served as Resident Coordinator in São Tomé and Príncipe and Angola, then moved to the UNDP Resident Representative role in the Central African Republic in 2025. The IIS identifies her as an alumna in its Ismaili studies programme.

Uganda and Canada · public service and community leadership

Salma Lakhani

born in Kampala · Lieutenant Governor since 2020

Born and raised in Kampala, Lakhani grew up in an Ismaili Muslim community and studied at Aga Khan School before completing an honours degree in clinical biochemistry at the University of Manchester. During Uganda’s 1972 expulsion of Asians she became stateless; she later settled in Edmonton with her husband in 1977, became a Canadian citizen and built a career in early-childhood education and volunteer service. She served many Alberta community organisations and became the province’s 19th Lieutenant Governor on 26 August 2020; she remains in office in the current official biography.
