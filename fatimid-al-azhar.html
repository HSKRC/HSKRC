Early history

A mosque founded in Fatimid Cairo

Construction of al-Azhar Mosque began in 970 CE (359 AH) under the direction of Jawhar al-Siqilli for the Fatimid imam-caliph al-Muʿizz li-Din Allah. The mosque was inaugurated in 972 CE (361 AH), becoming an important congregational mosque of the newly founded capital.

Institutional change

From congregational mosque to centre of learning

Al-Azhar began as a mosque and grew into a seat of teaching and scholarship. Its later history as a university should not be conflated with the date at which the original mosque was built. Under the Fatimids it became a setting for religious learning; subsequent dynasties reshaped its institutional character.

This distinction matters for public history: 970–972 refers to construction and inauguration of the mosque, not to a single universally agreed foundation date for the modern university.

Long-term heritage

An institution across centuries

Al-Azhar’s place in Cairo illustrates the lasting impact of Fatimid urban and intellectual patronage. Its teaching traditions continued and changed substantially under later rulers, particularly after the end of Fatimid rule in 1171.
